# Decision 0028: 0.4.0 holds Hodor in a service mesh: workloads proven by their certificates, decisions asked by the mesh, and an optional certificate authority

Status: Proposed (requested by the project owner)

## Context

At 0.3.0 a service or an agent has an identity of its own in a tenant: a service account with clients and API keys, holding roles like a person ([Milestone 8](../product/epics.md#milestone-8-workloads-agents-and-webhooks)). It proves who it is with a secret, a signed assertion or an API key that someone had to give it.

The services that most need such an identity often already have one. A service mesh gives every workload a certificate and proves it to every other workload over mutual TLS: Istio, Consul and Linkerd do, and SPIRE does for workloads outside a mesh. Istio, Consul and SPIRE name the workload with a SPIFFE identifier; Linkerd names it with a DNS name of its own. The mesh's proxies can also check a token and ask an outside service whether a request may pass. Today none of this reaches Hodor:

- a workload cannot prove who it is to Hodor with the certificate or the JWT-SVID its mesh gave it, so a second credential must be handed out and rotated;
- a mesh's proxy cannot ask Hodor's Authorization API, because the proxy speaks Envoy's external authorization protocol and the API does not;
- Linkerd, one of the most used meshes, checks neither tokens nor an outside decision, so it needs a path of its own;
- every mesh runs its own certificate authority, so two meshes of one tenant do not trust each other unless an operator makes them share a root by hand.

After 0.3.0 the project owner asked for Hodor to integrate with several service meshes, Linkerd included, and for the layer that issues certificates to be planned as a milestone too, as a feature a tenant turns on.

Keycloak accepts certificates and SPIFFE identities from outside and issues none. Ory Hydra does neither. See [the competitive analysis](../product/competitive-analysis.md#26-service-meshes-and-workload-certificates).

## Decision

- **0.4.0 is three milestones, built in this order:**
  1. [Milestone 10](../product/epics.md#milestone-10-workloads-prove-who-they-are-with-their-certificates): workloads prove who they are with their certificates.
  2. [Milestone 11](../product/epics.md#milestone-11-hodor-in-a-service-mesh): Hodor in a service mesh.
  3. [Milestone 12](../product/epics.md#milestone-12-an-optional-certificate-authority-per-tenant): an optional certificate authority per tenant.
- **The order follows what each part needs.**
  - Accepting certificates comes first because both later parts stand on it. The adapter of Milestone 11 names a workload by the SPIFFE identifier its certificate carries. The authority of Milestone 12 is worth something only once Hodor trusts what it signs.
  - The mesh comes before the authority because it is useful to every tenant with a mesh, while the authority is useful only to the tenants that turn it on.
- **How Hodor fits a mesh is [Decision 0029](0029-hodor-in-a-service-mesh.md):** Hodor accepts certificates and does not issue them to workloads. The proxies check Hodor's tokens. Decisions are asked through a separate binary, `hodor-ext-authz`. Linkerd gets policies written from Hodor's roles and a gateway in front of it.
- **The certificate authority is [Decision 0030](0030-optional-certificate-authority.md).** It is off unless the installation and the tenant both turn it on. It signs the intermediate certificates of the tenant's meshes, and only for the tenant's trust domains. It never signs a workload's own certificate.
- **Milestone 10 pulls forward what Phase 6 planned for mutual TLS clients** (Requirement-OAuth-14) and adds what the mesh needs: certificates forwarded by a trusted proxy (Requirement-OAuth-23), trust domains per tenant (Requirement-Workload-04) and SPIFFE client authentication (Requirement-Workload-05). Signing in a *person* with a client certificate (Requirement-Authentication-12) stays in Phase 6.
- **Milestone 11 pulls forward from Phase 8** the adapter for Envoy's external authorization (Requirement-Authorization-12) and the Linkerd policies (Requirement-Workload-06). It also makes Hodor run inside a mesh itself (Requirement-Operations-08).
- **Milestone 12 takes the authority of Requirement-Key-04 and the OpenBao key provider it needs (Requirement-Key-06), pulled forward from Phase 9.** The authority's key must never leave the provider that signs with it, and the local provider does not meet that. The cloud's key management services (Requirement-Key-03) fit the same port and keep Phase 9. Out:
  - certificates for each workload issued by Hodor;
  - short-lived certificates in exchange for an access token (Requirement-Key-05);
  - ACME, EST, CMP or SCEP servers;
  - certificates for people;
  - revocation lists and OCSP.
- **0.4.0 is released when Milestone 12 is done**, following [the release rules](../development/conventions.md#releases). Requirement-Key-04 is Optional. If Milestone 12 slips, the owner may release 0.4.0 with Milestones 10 and 11, and the authority moves to the next version.
- **The rules of the project stay.** The documentation of each issue comes first. Cross-tenant cases cover every new trust domain, certificate, assertion and signature. Each milestone ends with a demonstration that runs in continuous integration, on a kind cluster with the meshes it names.

## Consequences

- At 0.4.0 a workload in Istio, Consul, Linkerd or under SPIRE proves who it is to Hodor with the identity it already has. A proxy of those meshes checks Hodor's tokens and asks Hodor's decisions, and Linkerd gets the same through its own policies and a gateway.
- Hodor's continuous integration starts running service meshes. Each one tried costs build minutes and maintenance on every version of that mesh. Istio and Linkerd are tried in continuous integration. The other meshes are documented and marked as not tried until someone tries them.
- SPIFFE client authentication follows an Internet-Draft. Its version is pinned, and a change to the draft is a change to Hodor that the changelog states.
- With the authority on, an installation runs OpenBao beside Hodor. Hodor holds a key, inside OpenBao, that can make any workload of that tenant trusted by its meshes. That changes what Hodor is worth to an attacker. [Decision 0030](0030-optional-certificate-authority.md) says what protects it, and the [threat model](../security/threat-model.md#service-meshes-and-certificates) lists the threats.
- What is left out is not dropped. It keeps its phase, and the roadmap's phases keep their numbers.

## Alternatives considered

- **Within 0.3.0, as a Milestone 10 or as a slice of Milestone 8.** It would come sooner, since service accounts arrive in Milestone 8. Set aside because the owner kept 0.3.0 as planned, and because a slice this large would double Milestone 8.
- **Only accepting certificates, without the authority.** It covers most of what a mesh needs. Set aside because the owner asked for the authority to be planned, as a feature a tenant turns on.
- **The authority before the mesh.** Set aside: an authority that signs for meshes Hodor cannot yet talk to has no use.
