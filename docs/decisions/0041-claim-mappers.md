# Decision 0041: Claim mappers name their tokens, never a claim Hodor sets, and are bounded when they are saved

Status: Accepted

## Context

[Issue-99](../product/epics.md#slice-openid-connect-epic-10) asks for claim mappers per client: claims taken from what Hodor knows of a person, from their groups, from the organization they signed in for, or written as constants, checked when they are saved. A mapper may not name a claim Hodor sets, and a token has a bound on its size that is checked when a mapper is saved, not when a token is issued.

Hodor already writes its own claims: those of [the ID token](../architecture/contexts/oauth.md#the-id-token), of [the access token](../architecture/contexts/oauth.md#the-access-token) and of [UserInfo](../architecture/contexts/oauth.md#userinfo), the `groups` claim and the `org_id` of [Issue-89](../architecture/contexts/oauth.md#the-organization-of-a-sign-in), and the `act` of [token exchange](0034-token-exchange.md). A mapper adds to these; it must never change what Hodor says, and a token it grows must still fit in the headers that carry it. Seven questions had to be answered: which tokens a mapper writes into, what it may read, which names it may not use, how big it may make a token, whether a scope must be asked for, how mappers of one client meet, and what token exchange and introspection do with what mappers wrote.

## Decision

- **Each mapper names its targets**: one or more of the ID token, the access token and the answer of UserInfo. A claim meant for an application's own page goes into the ID token; one meant for its interfaces goes into the access token; one that should stay out of every token a browser holds is answered at UserInfo alone.
- **Five sources, each read when the token is issued:** an attribute of the person (display name, primary username, primary email address, whether it is verified, locale); the person's effective groups, as identifiers; whether the person is in one group, effectively, as a boolean; the organization of the sign-in, by identifier or by name; and a constant, any JSON value but `null`. What is read is the person's own, in the tenant of the token: groups and organizations come through the same port as the `groups` claim, the attributes through a port over the identity context, never another tenant's. A value the person does not have, such as an email address, or an organization when the sign-in named none, leaves the claim out.
- **Names Hodor sets cannot be mapped.** The reserved names are every claim Hodor writes in an ID token, an access token or an answer of UserInfo today, every member a specification Hodor follows gives a meaning to in those tokens, and the names other issues of this milestone write: `iss`, `sub`, `aud`, `exp`, `nbf`, `iat`, `jti`, `auth_time`, `nonce`, `acr`, `amr`, `azp`, `at_hash`, `c_hash`, `s_hash`, `sid`, `scope`, `client_id`, `cnf`, `act`, `may_act`, `events`, `org_id`, `groups`, `principal_kind`, `name`, `preferred_username`, `locale`, `updated_at`, `email`, `email_verified`, `_claim_names` and `_claim_sources`. They are compared without regard to case, so that `SUB` is refused with `sub`. A unit test builds every token Hodor writes, with every optional claim present, and checks that each name it holds is reserved, so that a claim Hodor starts writing cannot be forgotten here.
- **The bound is on what the mappers of one client add to each target together, 4096 bytes as JSON writes it, in the worst case, checked when a mapper is saved.** Each source has a largest value it can write: four bytes per character the identity context allows a text, plus the quotes (a display name of 200 characters is at most 802 bytes); 100 group identifiers (3901 bytes); `false` (5 bytes); an organization's identifier (38 bytes) or name (402 bytes); a constant's exact size, at most 1024 bytes. A mapper's worst case is its value's plus its name, the quotes, the colon and the comma. A mapper is refused, and nothing is stored, when the worst cases of every mapper of the client that writes into one target, the new or changed one included, would add up to more than 4096 bytes for that target. Since no value is ever written larger than its worst case, no token is ever refused or cut when it is issued: a value that would be longer, which only escaping or a locale longer than 35 characters can make, and a person in more than 100 groups, leave the claim out.
- **A scope is optional.** A mapper with a scope applies only when the scopes of the token hold it, and the scope must be one the client may ask for; without one, it applies to every token of its targets that the client gets for a person. A tenant whose consent should cover a claim gives its mapper a scope the consent page names.
- **One mapper per claim name in a client**, compared without regard to case, and at most 20 mappers per client. Two mappers of a client can therefore never write one claim, whatever their targets, and the order they are applied in changes nothing; Hodor applies them by claim name. A mapper only ever adds a claim Hodor did not write: its name is reserved if Hodor writes it.
- **Mappers apply to tokens about a person, issued to the client they belong to**: the ID token and the access token of a code, the access token of a refresh, and the answer of UserInfo to the client's access token. A token of client credentials carries none, since nobody signed in. **A token made by exchange carries none**, neither the mappers of the person's client nor those of the agent's: it is for another audience, issued to another client, and what the person's client was configured to receive is not the agent's to carry on. **Introspection answers none**: its answer is the fixed list of members it has always had, and a resource server that wants a mapped claim reads it from the token.

## Consequences

- An administrator can put a group into the ID token, which [the ID token](../architecture/contexts/oauth.md#the-id-token) otherwise keeps free of the person's claims. Doing so is the administrator's choice for one client, and the claim is in a token the browser may hold.
- A mapper that reads the person's groups or attributes reads them at every issue, so a person taken out of a group loses the claim from the next token, as with the `groups` claim. Tokens already issued keep what they say until they expire.
- Saving a mapper reads the client and its other mappers under a lock of the client's row, so that two administrators adding mappers at the same moment cannot pass the bound together.
- The discovery document does not list mapped claims: they are a client's, not the tenant's.
- Custom attributes of a person do not exist yet. When they do, they become another kind of attribute, with the bound their definition gives.

## Alternatives considered

- **Checking the size when a token is issued, and refusing or cutting the token.** Set aside: the issue asks for the bound to be met at save time, and a sign-in that fails because of a person's long name is found by the person, not by the administrator who caused it.
- **Mappers that may replace a claim Hodor sets**, such as `sub` or `groups`. Set aside: a resource server trusts those claims for what Hodor says they mean, and a mapper that could change `sub` or `acr` could make one person look like another, or a weak sign-in look strong.
- **An order of mappers, with a later one winning a claim name.** Set aside for the simpler rule of one mapper per name: there is nothing to order and nothing to explain.
- **Mappers on the access token of a token exchange.** Set aside as above; an agent's client that needs a claim asks the resource server or reads it from the person's token.
- **Group names in place of identifiers.** Set aside for the reason [the `groups` claim](../architecture/contexts/oauth.md#the-groups-claim) gives: a name can change, and a token issued before would then name another group, or none. The boolean source covers a claim about one group by name in the application.
