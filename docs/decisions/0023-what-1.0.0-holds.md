# Decision 0023: 1.0.0 holds the OAuth server, strong authentication and the authorization engine

Status: Proposed (the three parts were requested by the project owner; the scope of each waits for their review)

## Context

After Milestones 1 and 2, Hodor lets people into a tenant but cannot tell an application who they are. The project owner chose to release 1.0.0 only once three parts are built:

- the OAuth 2.x authorization server and OpenID Provider (Phase 2 of the [roadmap](../product/roadmap.md));
- multi-factor authentication with passkeys and step-up (part of Phase 4);
- the authorization engine with policies and Open Policy Agent (part of Phase 5).

The work already built was released as 0.1.0, a pre-release. In the roadmap, Phases 4 and 5 hold much more than these three parts, and together with Phase 2 they weigh about five times Phase 1. Building all of it before 1.0.0 would delay the release with work nobody asked for yet. Each of the three parts has to be cut to what makes it whole.

## Decision

- **1.0.0 is three milestones, built in this order:** [Milestone 3](../product/epics.md#milestone-3-applications-ask-hodor-who-someone-is), the OAuth server and OpenID Provider; [Milestone 4](../product/epics.md#milestone-4-strong-authentication), multi-factor authentication, passkeys and step-up; [Milestone 5](../product/epics.md#milestone-5-the-authorization-engine), the authorization engine with Open Policy Agent. The order follows what each part needs. Step-up is asked for with the `acr_values` of OpenID Connect. The Authorization API is called with access tokens, and its roles are scoped to the applications of Milestone 3.
- **Milestone 3 is all of Phase 2 except three requirements marked Expected:** pairwise subject identifiers (Requirement-OAuth-10), claim mappers per client (Requirement-OAuth-21) and back-channel logout (part of Requirement-OAuth-11). Logout initiated by the relying party is in. The exit criterion of Phase 2 stays: the Basic OpenID Provider and Config OpenID Provider profiles of the OpenID conformance suite pass in continuous integration.
- **Milestone 3 moves the secrets of Milestone 2 under managed keys.** [Decision 0017](0017-external-identities-before-oauth.md#consequences) left a secret encryption keyed from configuration; the key domain of this milestone takes it over, as that decision foresaw.
- **Milestone 4 takes from Phase 4 only what a second factor and step-up need.** In:
  - TOTP;
  - WebAuthn as a second factor;
  - passkeys as the first and only step;
  - single-use recovery codes;
  - conditional and optional steps in the flow engine, so that a tenant can require a second factor;
  - assurance levels mapped to `acr` and `amr`;
  - step-up that completes only the missing steps;
  - the pages where a person manages their own factors.

  Out:
  - HOTP;
  - magic link and one-time passwords by email or text message;
  - risk signals and trusted devices;
  - the flow editing interface;
  - session limits;
  - pushed authorization requests, DPoP, the device grant and dynamic client registration;
  - the rest of the account console.
- **Milestone 5 takes from Phase 5 the engine and Open Policy Agent, not the identities of workloads.** In:
  - the engine contract of [Decision 0009](0009-authorization-engine-contract.md);
  - native role-based access control with hierarchy and composite roles;
  - attribute-based access control conditions;
  - the Authorization API with evaluation, batches and simulation;
  - the decision log;
  - the Open Policy Agent adapter, bundles and the policy lifecycle of [Decision 0010](0010-opa-integration.md).

  Out:
  - per-resource access control lists;
  - relationship-based access control;
  - token exchange, service accounts, agents and API keys;
  - webhooks.
- **Rego written by a tenant runs only in an Open Policy Agent instance the operator runs for that tenant.** Decision 0010 already requires a dedicated instance. In 1.0.0 Hodor does not start such instances; the operator registers one with its bundle credential.
- **Roles are assigned to users and to clients only.** Groups and organizations do not exist yet, so assigning a role to them waits until they do.
- **The consoles follow only where they must.** The admin console gains applications, clients, keys, roles, policies and bindings; signing in to the console with OpenID Connect stays out ([Decision 0020](0020-console-on-the-hodor-session.md) holds).
- **The rules of the project stay.** For each issue the documentation comes first, and cross-tenant cases cover every new token, code and table. Each milestone ends with a demonstration that runs in continuous integration.

## Consequences

- At 1.0.0 an application signs a person in through Hodor, can require a second factor or a passkey, and asks Hodor whether that person may do something. That covers the functional scope the [product requirements](../product/product-requirements.md#9-release-criteria) give to Beta, without the workload identities and with roles limited to users and clients.
- The number 1.0.0 is not general availability. High availability, a disaster recovery drill, an external pentest and the external review of SAML stay in Phase 9. The changelog of 1.0.0 says so, as it does for 0.1.0.
- What is left out above is not dropped. It keeps its phase, and the roadmap's phases keep their numbers.
- The milestones depend on each other. A change of mind about Milestone 3, such as opaque tokens by default, reaches the two after it.

## Alternatives considered

- **All of Phases 2, 4 and 5 before 1.0.0.** That puts about five times Phase 1 of work before the release, half of it on requirements marked Expected or Optional. Set aside: it puts off what the owner asked for in order to build what nobody has asked for yet.
- **The three parts in parallel.** Step-up and the Authorization API would be built against an OAuth server that does not exist yet, and both would be reworked when it arrives.
- **Authorization before multi-factor authentication.** It is possible, since the engine needs access tokens but not strong authentication. Set aside because the owner chose this order.
